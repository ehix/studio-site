# Studio naming — parked 2026-08-04

Deferred until the five directions exist, so candidates can be seen set in five
typographic treatments rather than judged on a list. Placeholder in the
meantime: wordmark `STUDIO —`, domain `studio.example`, single source per
version, sized for a real name of 6–14 characters.

**No availability or existing-trader checks have been run on any of these.**

## Brief

Design · human · competency · care · technology capability. Airy and
open-minded, not clipped or technical. Calm and confident, never loud SaaS
hype. A founder should think *"this person actually understands technology,
design and data"* within three seconds.

## Front runner

**Idiom** — *idiomatic* is a term of art in both engineering and design. In
code it is the highest praise: written the way the language wants to be
written, by someone who stopped translating. In design it is the visual
vernacular someone speaks natively rather than assembles from rules. Anyone can
be correct; almost nobody is idiomatic. Warm, human, three open syllables, no
commercial reading.

**Identity play, works for any language-derived name:** set the wordmark as a
dictionary specimen entry — pronunciation, part of speech, definition — as
marginalia about the studio's own name. Same move as the region markers on the
Cyrillic Swiss poster in the taste library: notation applied to the artefact
itself.

```
Idiom  ˈɪdɪəm  n.
    the form of a language natural
    to its native speakers
```

## Ruled out, with reasons

- **Datum, Detent, Deckle, Register, Vernier** — too brutal. Clipped, hard
  consonants, instrument-precision. Read as "measuring device", not "room to
  think".
- **Openwork, Tracery, Clerestory** — architectural thread, chased off the
  Malmesbury Abbey project. Wrong: the taste library is overwhelmingly
  *nature*-derived, not architectural — 27 titles across weather, open ground,
  growth and water.
- **Thermal, Understory, Field, Tideline** — nature-derived and good, but the
  brief is not bound to nature and these narrow it.
- **Margin** — genuinely elegant, since the whole visual language is
  marginalia and the name would have made identity and design system the same
  decision. Killed by the profit-margin echo.
- **Native** — sharp double meaning (fluent speaker, native to the platform)
  but carries indigenous connotations that vary by market, and is a heavily
  used tech word.
