# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective clients first, peers second.

Clients are people with something to build — a website, a game, a publication —
who are deciding whether this studio is worth an email. They arrive knowing
little, they are scanning rather than reading, and they are trying to answer
two questions quickly: *can this person actually do the thing*, and *would I
want to work with them*. Success is an enquiry.

Peers are other designers and developers. They are not the reason the site
exists, but the site has to survive their attention: it should be good enough
that someone who builds things for a living respects the craft. Where the two
audiences conflict, the client wins.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio for a studio that makes websites, games and publications. The
design is the product: the site is itself the strongest available proof of
capability, so anything sloppy on it is a direct argument against hiring.

It exists to convert a stranger's attention into a conversation. Success is a
real enquiry from someone with real work; secondary success is being shared by
other designers.

## Brand Personality

**Quiet, considered, confident.**

Understated and self-assured. Lets the work carry the argument rather than
claiming things about itself. Never oversells, never shouts, never pads. The
tone is a capable person describing what they do plainly — warmth comes from
evident care and precision, not from friendliness in the copy.

Closest in feel to Prisma (sitesplaced.com/s/demo-prisma) of the four
references supplied: generous space, cinematic imagery, roman and italic mixed
inside a single sentence, layered dark surfaces, almost no interface furniture.

## Anti-references

- **Loud brutalism.** Clashing colour, hard borders everywhere, monospace
  shouting, deliberate ugliness as a position. Explicitly ruled out by the
  user. This also rules out most of what 2xa.studio does, despite it being one
  of the supplied references — take its *scale contrast*, not its volume.
- **Specimen sheets and technical dossiers.** Marginalia, live readouts, plate
  numbers, dashed placeholder boxes, everything annotated. Not user-rejected,
  but it is this project's demonstrated failure mode: ten consecutive attempts
  produced it by reflex. Treat as a known trap rather than a rule.
- **The big-serif minimal portfolio.** Enormous serif wordmark, vast
  whitespace, three project rows, nothing else. Adjacent to the target and easy
  to fall into by accident; the difference is that this site must be a full
  page that does a job, not a hero with a stub attached.

## Design Principles

1. **The work is the argument.** Show the thing, don't describe it. Every claim
   the site makes about capability should be visible on the page rather than
   asserted in copy.

2. **Quiet is not empty.** Restraint means fewer, better elements at generous
   scale — not a thin page. The reference sites run 5,600 to 14,000px with 8 to
   14 sections that each do a job. Length comes from substance, never padding.

3. **Design in the library's language, don't depict its subjects.** The taste
   library is 102 captures of instruments, paper, notation and screens. Its
   value is shape, colour, texture, type setting, layout and material — those
   become the site's own design language. Putting a picture of an instrument on
   the page is the category error this project keeps making.

4. **Nothing on the page may be untrue.** No invented metrics, clients, dates
   or coordinates. Where a value is missing, either omit the element or make
   the gap obviously a gap. This constraint is permanent and applies to
   placeholder states as much as to shipped copy.

5. **Earn every effect.** Motion, texture and imagery each need a reason beyond
   decoration. If an effect can be removed without loss, remove it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

**Proposed default, not yet confirmed by the user** — correct this in a line if
it's wrong:

- WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, verified rather than assumed.
- Full `prefers-reduced-motion` support: every animation needs a crossfade or
  instant alternative, and the page must be complete and legible with motion
  disabled.
- Content never gated behind a scroll-triggered reveal, so it survives
  headless renderers, hidden tabs and reader modes.
- Keyboard reachable throughout with visible focus states.
- Colour never the sole carrier of meaning.
