What this is

One camera, one world, one sun, one weather, for the whole page. It opens low over the range and flies. Scrolling takes it straight up off the flight line and pitches it over into a survey; the page you are reading floats on that. Further down it descends back down the same column and turns to finish on the wordmark in the grass, facing whichever way has the most high ground on it.

It goes up and down over one spot rather than travelling to a chosen one. An earlier cut searched the terrain for the best place to land and flew there during the climb, which guaranteed a good backdrop and was thoroughly disorientating — a climb and a long sideways traverse at the same time, with no way to read either against the other. The backdrop is guaranteed by turning instead. A turn is a motion you can follow.

There is a water table, not a lake: one level, and wherever the ground falls below it there is water. That costs a divide per ray rather than a surface to march, and it pays for itself — nothing under the surface is visible, so the crossing is a hard draw-distance cap.

The last part of the descent slides across to a clearing. On a generated map, two thirds of all spots have ground above head height within sixty units, and landing on one of those puts the camera inside a hillside — the frame fills with near ground and there is no horizon in it at all. So the flight looks around on the way down and puts itself on open, level ground with something to see. Median move: a hundred and sixty units, while it is already falling.

The hero renderer could not do this. It is a Voxel Space column marcher, fast because it assumes every screen column is a vertical plane in the world, which is true at zero pitch and at no other angle. This needed eighty degrees, so it is a per-pixel march instead. What keeps that affordable: for a heightfield seen from above, hit distance falls monotonically down a column, so each pixel starts from the one above it and lands in two or three steps.

The rail in the margin is the instruments, and it flies the page as well as reporting on it: drag the tape, or pick a phase by name. The camera trails the scrollbar rather than tracking it, so a shove on the wheel arrives as an acceleration and a settle rather than a lurch.
