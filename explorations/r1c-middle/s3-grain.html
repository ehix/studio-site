What this is

No new imagery — the dither cell size becomes the scale cue. Coarse at the top of the page, fine at the bottom, so the material resolves as you descend, the way anything does when you get closer to it. It costs one number per graphic and no artwork at all.

The strip below shows the same hillside at every step of the ladder at once, so the effect can be judged without scrolling 5,000 pixels.

The band further down is the version that answers “something subtle in the background that develops as we scroll”: its cell size is driven by its own position in the viewport, so the dither literally resolves as it rises. It gets cheaper as it approaches, not dearer — the buffer shrinks.
