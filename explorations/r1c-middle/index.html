Five ideas for the middle, each built as a working page.

The page reads as a descent: a hundred square kilometres of landscape at the top, a single blade of grass at the bottom. Nothing in between currently knows that. These five are ways of making the middle carry it.

How to read these

Each page runs r1b’s actual hero and sign-off, loaded from ../r1b-landscape/, so what you see is the real machinery rather than a copy that has quietly drifted. r1b itself is untouched apart from three additive exports: the heightmap, the gust field, and an extra-tab hook on the tuning panel.

Press ` on any of them for the sliders. Each showcase adds its own tab alongside hero and sign-off.

The work plates are still placeholders throughout. They are waiting for real screenshots and none of these ideas touch them.

s1

The same place, surveyed

The middle of the page is a contour map and an elevation profile of the terrain the hero is flying over. The same heightmap, not terrain-like imagery. The crosshair on the map is the camera, and it moves, because the hero is still flying.

cost near zero · the 885k Perlin evaluations were already paid at load

s2

Altitude in the margin

A hairline in the gutter carrying a height that falls as you read: 1,240m over the range, 8cm in the grass. No new graphic at all. It does not illustrate the descent, it states it, in the margins where the brief puts terminal flavour.

cost nothing · DOM and a scroll listener

s3

The grain resolves

Dither cell size as the scale cue: coarse at the top of the page, fine at the bottom, so the material resolves as you descend. One band drives its cell size from its own scroll position and literally resolves as it rises.

cost negative · the buffer shrinks as you approach

s4

One weather system

One gust field shared by the hero’s haze, a mid-page band and the grass, sampled at a depth so a gust arrives later further down. The page becomes one afternoon in one place. A trace in the corner lets you watch the gust travel.

cost one band · it drives published params, patches nothing

s5

One descending camera

A single fixed canvas behind the whole document, descending through ridge, hillside, tussock and stem as you scroll. The purest form of the idea and the one I argued against building first. Built anyway, so the argument can be had against something real.

cost high · fights the type all the way down; note the slabs it needs

Where I’d land

s1 and s2 together. They are the two cheapest things on the list, they reinforce each other, and between them they convert the descent from something a reader might notice into something the page says. s3 is close to free and composes with either. s4 is the one that makes the page feel alive rather than assembled. s5 is worth having seen and worth not shipping yet.
